# Brainstorming Skill

## Overview

This skill provides structured techniques and methodologies for effective brainstorming sessions. Whether you're generating ideas for a project, solving complex problems, or exploring creative possibilities, this skill offers proven approaches to maximize idea generation and quality.

## When to Use

- Starting a new project and need initial ideas
- Facing a complex problem requiring creative solutions
- Looking to improve or innovate on existing solutions
- Exploring multiple approaches before committing to a direction
- Team needs to align on possibilities and directions
- Feeling stuck and need fresh perspectives
- Preparing for innovation workshops or design sprints

Don't use for:
- When you need a single, definitive answer quickly
- Situations requiring deep technical analysis (use spike or concept-exploration instead)
- When time is extremely limited (<5 minutes for meaningful output)
- Situations requiring authoritative decision-making without exploration

## Core Principles

1. **Quantity over quality initially** - Generate many ideas before filtering
2. **Defer judgment** - Withhold criticism during idea generation phase
3. **Build on others' ideas** - Use stimulation and combination techniques
4. **Encourage wild ideas** - Extreme ideas can lead to practical breakthroughs
5. **Stay focused on the topic** - Keep sessions targeted to the problem space

## Brainstorming Techniques

### 1. Brainwriting (6-3-5 Method)
- 6 participants, 3 ideas each, 5 minutes per round
- Pass papers around, build on others' ideas
- Generates 108 ideas in 30 minutes
- Best for: Equal participation, reducing dominance by loud voices

### 2. SCAMPER Technique
- Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse
- Systematic questioning to transform existing ideas
- Best for: Improving existing products/processes

### 3. Mind Mapping
- Visual hierarchical representation of ideas
- Start with central concept, branch out with associations
- Best for: Exploring complex topics, showing relationships

### 4. Reverse Brainstorming
- Ask "How could we cause the problem?" instead of "How do we solve it?"
- Then reverse the solutions
- Best for: Identifying root causes, prevention strategies

### 5. Random Word Association
- Introduce random words to spark new connections
- Forces brain to make novel associations
- Best for: Breaking fixed thinking patterns, generating novel ideas

### 6. Role Storming
- Approach problem from different perspectives (customer, competitor, child, alien, etc.)
- Best for: Gaining new perspectives, identifying blind spots

### 7. Starbursting
- Focus on generating questions rather than answers
- Who, what, when, where, why, how questions about the topic
- Best for: Ensuring comprehensive exploration before solution generation

## Facilitation Guide

### Preparation (10-15 minutes)
1. **Define the challenge clearly** - Write a specific, actionable problem statement
2. **Select participants** - 4-8 people optimal for diverse thinking
3. **Choose technique** - Match technique to goal and group dynamics
4. **Prepare materials** - Whiteboards, sticky notes, markers, timer
5. **Set ground rules** - No judgment, build on ideas, stay focused, one conversation at a time

### Execution (20-40 minutes)
1. **Warm-up (5 min)** - Icebreaker or quick creative exercise
2. **Present challenge (2 min)** - Clear problem statement
3. **Idea generation (15-25 min)** - Apply chosen technique
4. **Clarification (5 min)** - Group similar ideas, ask clarifying questions
5. **Initial voting (5 min)** - Dot voting or silent selection of top ideas

### Post-Session (10-15 minutes)
1. **Capture all ideas** - Photograph, transcribe, or digitize output
2. **Initial clustering** - Group similar themes together
3. **Next steps planning** - Assign owners for idea exploration
4. **Feedback collection** - Quick retrospective on the process

## Individual Brainstorming Techniques

When working alone, use these adaptations:

### 1. Solo SCAMPER
- Systematically apply SCAMPER questions to your challenge
- Write down answers for each letter

### 2. Timer Method
- Set timer for 5-minute bursts
- Generate as many ideas as possible in each burst
- Take 2-minute breaks between bursts

### 3. Perspective Shifting
- Every 10 minutes, adopt a different perspective
- How would [expert/child/competitor] approach this?

### 4. Idea Quota
- Set a minimum number of ideas (e.g., 50) before stopping
- Pushes past obvious solutions to more creative ones

## Idea Evaluation & Selection

After generation, use these methods to identify promising ideas:

### Dot Voting
- Each person gets 3-5 dots to allocate to favorite ideas
- Reveals group consensus quickly

### Impact/Effort Matrix
- Plot ideas on 2x2 axis: Impact (low-high) vs Effort (low-high)
- Focus on high-impact, low-effort ("quick wins") first

### Three Lenses of Innovation
- Desirability (human/customer value)
- Feasibility (technical/operational viability)
- Viability (business/sustainability)
- Ideas scoring well on all three are strongest

### Weighted Scoring
- Define criteria (novelty, feasibility, impact, alignment)
- Weight each criterion by importance
- Score each option, calculate weighted totals

## Common Pitfalls & Solutions

### 1. Premature Criticism
- **Problem**: Judging ideas too early kills creativity
- **Solution**: Strictly enforce "no judgment" during generation phase
- **Solution**: Use a "parking lot" for concerns to address later

### 2. Dominant Personalities
- **Problem**: Loud voices dominate, quieter members withdraw
- **Solution**: Use structured techniques like brainwriting or round-robin
- **Solution**: Explicitly invite quieter members to contribute

### 3. Lack of Focus
- **Problem**: Ideas drift far from original problem
- **Solution**: Keep problem statement visible throughout
- **Solution**: Gentle facilitation to redirect when needed

### 4. Evaluation Apprehension
- **Problem**: People hold back due to fear of judgment
- **Solution**: Anonymous idea submission (especially early stages)
- **Solution**: Leader models vulnerability by sharing "silly" ideas first

### 5. Idea Fatigue
- **Problem**: Energy and idea quality decline over time
- **Solution**: Short, focused sessions (25-40 minutes max)
- **Solution**: Incorporate movement, breaks, or changes of medium

## One-Shot Recipes

### Quick Team Energizer (15 minutes)
**When**: Need quick ideas to break stagnation
**Technique**: Rapid fire round-robin
1. State problem clearly (2 min)
2. Go around circle, each person shares one idea (8 min)
3. Build on previous person's idea if inspired
4. Capture all ideas, dot vote for top 3 (5 min)

### Problem Re-framing Session (20 minutes)
**When**: Stuck on how to approach a problem
**Technique**: Reverse brainstorming + SCAMPER
1. State problem as "How might we..." (2 min)
2. Spend 5 minutes brainstorming how to CAUSE the problem
3. Reverse each "cause" into a potential preventive solution (8 min)
4. Apply SCAMPER to top 3 preventive ideas (5 min)
5. Vote on most promising approaches (5 min)

### Deep Dive Exploration (45 minutes)
**When**: Starting new project needing comprehensive exploration
**Technique**: Starbursting → Brainwriting → Clustering
1. Starbursting: Generate questions about the topic (10 min)
2. Brainwriting: 6-3-5 rounds to answer questions (20 min)
3. Clustering: Group answers into themes, identify patterns (10 min)
4. Dot vote on most promising theme areas (5 min)

## Verification Checklist

When completing a brainstorming session using this skill, verify:
- [ ] Clear problem statement was established and visible
- [ ] Appropriate technique selected for goals and group size
- [ ] Judgment was deferred during idea generation phase
- [ ] All participants had opportunity to contribute
- [ ] Ideas were captured comprehensively
- [ ] Some form of grouping/clustering was applied
- [ ] Selection/voting method was used to identify promising ideas
- [ ] Next steps and owners were assigned for follow-up
- [ ] Session reflection captured process improvements

## Memory and Reinforcement

After each session, spend 3 minutes reflecting individually:
1. What was the most surprising idea that emerged?
2. Which technique element worked best for our group?
3. What hindered our flow, and how can we address it next time?
4. What's one thing I'll try differently in our next session?

Track which techniques work best for different types of challenges and team compositions to build your facilitation expertise over time.
